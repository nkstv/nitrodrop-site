The realistic order of operations

1

Build an audience first — usually with your own money

Casinos sponsor exposure, not potential. Most streamers who now have affiliate deals started by playing with personal funds and streaming consistently until they had a real following to offer. Skipping straight to "get sponsored" rarely works.

2

Pick a casino and apply through its affiliate program

Stake is the most common starting point given its direct ties to Kick, but Rainbet, DegenCity, and others run their own affiliate programs too. Applications typically ask for your channel stats and average viewership — this is the point where an existing audience actually matters.

3

Understand what you're actually being offered

Deals vary a lot. Some are a flat fee regardless of results — the streamer gets paid whether they win or lose on stream. Others are revenue-share, tied to how much referred players deposit and wager. Bigger names tend toward flat fees; smaller and mid-size streamers are more likely to start on revenue share.

Set realistic expectations

For every streamer pulling in tens of millions a year, there are many more with real, engaged audiences and no meaningful sponsorship income at all — some profiles on this site explicitly have no net worth estimate because there's genuinely nothing to estimate yet. A following doesn't automatically convert into a deal, and most streamers who do land one make far less than the handful of names that get written about.

Before promoting any casino to an audience: gambling advertising law varies by country and region, and platforms differ in what they allow. Check the actual rules that apply to you and your viewers rather than assuming — this isn't optional homework, it's the difference between a legitimate channel and a legal problem.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need followers before a casino will sponsor me?

Almost always, yes. Casinos are paying for exposure, so they want proof an audience already exists before offering a code or deal. Streamers with no following typically start by playing with their own money and building a channel first, not the other way around.

How much can you actually make as a casino affiliate streamer?

It ranges from nothing to tens of millions a year, and most streamers land far closer to the low end. Several profiles on this site cover streamers with real, engaged audiences who still have no confirmed sponsorship income at all — a following doesn't automatically mean a deal exists.

Is it legal to stream casino affiliate content?

It depends entirely on where you and your viewers are located, and which casino you're promoting. Gambling advertising law varies enormously by country and even by region — this isn't something to guess at, and it's worth checking local rules specifically before promoting any casino to an audience.
