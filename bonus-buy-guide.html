What Is a Bonus Buy?

If you've watched any casino stream for more than a few minutes, you've probably seen a streamer pay upfront to skip straight into a slot's bonus round. Here's what that actually means, and why it's become the backbone of casino streaming content.

The basic idea

Most slots have a bonus round — free spins, a multiplier feature, something with real upside — that normally triggers randomly as you play. A bonus buy lets you skip the wait entirely: pay a set multiple of your bet, and the bonus round starts immediately, no spinning required to get there.

What it actually costs

Pricing varies by slot, but a bonus buy typically runs somewhere between 50x and 150x your base bet. A $1 spin might mean a $75-$150 buy-in just to enter the bonus round — the game then plays out from there like any other bonus round would, win or lose.

Why the odds aren't always the same

On a meaningful number of slots, the return-to-player percentage for a bought bonus is set slightly lower than for one triggered naturally through the base game. It's the tradeoff for guaranteed access to the highest-potential part of the slot — you're not gambling on whether the bonus happens anymore, just on how it plays out once you're in it.

Why streamers lean on it so heavily

Content pacing, mostly. Waiting for a bonus round to trigger naturally can take hundreds of spins with nothing happening on screen — dead time that doesn't make for watchable streaming. Buying straight into bonus rounds keeps the highlights coming at a predictable rate, which is exactly what most casino stream formats are built around.
