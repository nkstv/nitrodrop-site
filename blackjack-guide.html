How to Play Blackjack

Blackjack has the reputation of being a "smart" casino game, and it earns that reputation — unlike most games of pure chance, the decisions you make during a hand genuinely change your odds. Here's what actually matters.

The goal

Get your card total closer to 21 than the dealer, without going over. Number cards are worth their face value, face cards (J/Q/K) are worth 10, and an Ace is worth 1 or 11 — whichever helps your hand more. Beat the dealer's total, or have them bust past 21, and you win.

Your options on each turn

  • Hit — take another card, hoping to get closer to 21 without busting.
  • Stand — keep your current total and let the dealer play out their hand.
  • Double — double your bet in exchange for exactly one more card, then automatically stand. Usually done on strong starting totals like 10 or 11.

Why the dealer's up-card changes everything

Before you decide anything, look at the dealer's visible card. A dealer showing a 4, 5, or 6 is statistically more likely to bust, so it's often correct to stand on a mediocre total and let them take the risk. A dealer showing a 9, 10, or Ace is a strong hand — you generally need to take more risk yourself to keep up. This single habit — reading the dealer's card before deciding — is the biggest gap between casual play and informed play.

Frequently asked questions

Is online Blackjack fair?

Each hand is dealt from a freshly shuffled deck generated at random — there's no memory of previous hands carrying over, and no adjustment based on how much you've won or lost so far.

What is basic strategy?

Basic strategy is a set of mathematically-derived rules for when to hit, stand, or double, based only on your hand total and the dealer's up-card. Following it consistently is the single biggest lever a player has over the house edge — it doesn't guarantee wins, but it avoids the costly mistakes casual players make by guessing.

Does card counting work in online Blackjack?

Card counting relies on tracking which cards have already left a physical shoe to predict what's left in it. Online Blackjack typically reshuffles every hand, so there's no depleting shoe to track — the technique doesn't apply the way it does at a physical table.
